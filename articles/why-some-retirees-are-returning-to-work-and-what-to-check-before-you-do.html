Why Some Retirees Are Returning to Work—and What to Check Before You Do

Rising household costs are pushing some retirees to consider part-time or full-time work again.

The Washington Post reported that more retirees are returning to paid work as living costs and unexpected expenses put pressure on retirement budgets. Going back to work can help cash flow, but it can also affect taxes, Social Security benefits for people below full retirement age, and Medicare-related costs in some circumstances.

What to keep in mind

  • Calculate the extra income after taxes.
  • Check Social Security earnings rules if you have not reached full retirement age.
  • Consider how wages affect your overall tax picture.
  • Include commuting and work-related costs.
  • Try a part-time schedule before making a full career change.
A useful rule: Slow down when a headline asks you to act immediately. Verify the source, check the date and look for the original agency, research paper or provider information.

The bigger picture

Because Social Security and tax outcomes depend on individual circumstances, consider a qualified professional for a personal decision.
