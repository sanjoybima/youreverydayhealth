Aging in Place Is Popular—But the House May Need to Change

Most older adults want to remain at home, but staying independent can require planning before a crisis forces a rushed decision.

Pew found that 93% of adults 65 and older who live at home say they live in their own home or apartment, and many want to remain there. Aging in place is easier when changes are made early: better lighting, safer bathrooms, fewer stairs, accessible storage and a plan for transportation.

What to keep in mind

  • Walk through the home looking for trip hazards.
  • Consider grab bars and bathroom safety improvements.
  • Keep important items at reachable heights.
  • Plan how you would manage if driving became difficult.
  • Discuss future needs with family before an emergency.
A useful rule: Slow down when a headline asks you to act immediately. Verify the source, check the date and look for the original agency, research paper or provider information.

The bigger picture

Home modifications should reflect an individual's mobility and medical needs; consult qualified professionals when necessary.
