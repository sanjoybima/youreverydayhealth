The Retirement Emergency Fund: Why Six Months May Not Tell the Whole Story

A retirement emergency fund has to cover more than ordinary living expenses because some large costs are difficult to predict.

A practical emergency reserve can include separate buckets for household repairs, medical or dental costs, insurance deductibles and family emergencies. The right amount varies by income, assets and expenses. The key is to avoid forcing every unexpected bill onto a credit card or a long-term investment account.

What to keep in mind

  • Estimate your largest realistic surprise expense.
  • Keep emergency savings accessible.
  • Separate short-term reserves from long-term investments.
  • Replenish the reserve after a large withdrawal.
  • Review the amount annually.
A useful rule: Slow down when a headline asks you to act immediately. Verify the source, check the date and look for the original agency, research paper or provider information.

The bigger picture

There is no universal emergency-fund number for retirees. A financial professional can help tailor one to your situation.
