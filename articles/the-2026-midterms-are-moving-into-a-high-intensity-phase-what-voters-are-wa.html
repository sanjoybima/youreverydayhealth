The 2026 Midterms Are Moving Into a High-Intensity Phase: What Voters Are Watching

With the November elections approaching, national attention is turning toward competitive Senate and House contests and the issues most likely to shape turnout.

Recent national coverage has focused on competitive Senate races, campaign spending and shifting polling in several states. For readers over 55, elections can feel especially personal because federal and state decisions affect Medicare, Social Security, taxes, prescription costs, housing and local health services. The best way to follow the race is to focus on concrete proposals rather than campaign drama alone.

What to keep in mind

  • Check your state election office for voting rules.
  • Read candidate positions directly when possible.
  • Watch debates or full interviews instead of relying only on clips.
  • Verify claims about federal benefits with official agencies.
  • Make a simple list of the issues that matter most to your household.
A useful rule: Slow down when a headline asks you to act immediately. Verify the source, check the date and look for the original agency, research paper or provider information.

The bigger picture

Polling is a snapshot, not a prediction. Election outcomes and policy consequences should not be inferred from a single poll or headline.
