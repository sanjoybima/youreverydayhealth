The Brain-Health Conversation Is Changing for Adults 55 and Older

Brain health is moving beyond memory alone, with more attention on sleep, physical activity, social connection, hearing and cardiovascular risk.

Aging research increasingly treats brain health as part of overall health. A practical approach is to focus on habits that support the body and mind together: staying physically active, managing blood pressure and other conditions, getting adequate sleep, staying socially connected and discussing hearing or vision changes with a clinician.

What to keep in mind

  • Treat blood pressure and other risk factors seriously.
  • Keep physically active within your abilities.
  • Stay socially and mentally engaged.
  • Address hearing or vision changes.
  • Talk to a clinician about persistent memory or thinking changes.
A useful rule: Slow down when a headline asks you to act immediately. Verify the source, check the date and look for the original agency, research paper or provider information.

The bigger picture

There is no single routine that prevents dementia. Persistent or concerning symptoms deserve professional evaluation.
