Banking and Scam Protection: Five Habits Worth Adopting After 55

As more older adults manage money online, simple security habits can reduce the chance of falling for common scams.

AARP research shows that older adults are increasingly active online, while confidence with complex or risky digital tasks can vary. The answer is not avoiding technology; it is using a few repeatable safeguards. Treat unexpected urgency as a warning sign, especially when someone asks for money, gift cards, passwords or one-time codes.

What to keep in mind

  • Turn on multi-factor authentication.
  • Use unique passwords for financial accounts.
  • Never share one-time verification codes.
  • Call a bank using the number on your card, not a caller's number.
  • Pause when someone creates artificial urgency.
A useful rule: Slow down when a headline asks you to act immediately. Verify the source, check the date and look for the original agency, research paper or provider information.

The bigger picture

No security method eliminates all risk. If you suspect fraud, contact your financial institution promptly.
