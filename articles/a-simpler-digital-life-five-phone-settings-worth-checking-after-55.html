A Simpler Digital Life: Five Phone Settings Worth Checking After 55

A few basic settings can make a smartphone less distracting and more secure.

Smartphones can become easier to use when notifications are reduced and security features are turned on. Start with screen size and text settings, emergency contacts, automatic updates, two-factor authentication and call-filtering tools where available. What to keep in mind Increase text size if reading is uncomfortable. Enable automatic software updates. Add emergency contacts. Turn on multi-factor authentication for important accounts. Technology is no longer a separate part of life

AARP's 2026 technology research found that smartphone ownership among adults 50-plus reached 90% in 2025. Older adults are using technology for communication, finances, health information, travel and entertainment. The question is increasingly how to make that technology easier and safer.

Start with readability

A larger text size, stronger contrast and fewer unnecessary notifications can make a phone less tiring. If you find yourself repeatedly zooming or searching for the same setting, spend ten minutes changing the device to suit you rather than adapting yourself to its default settings.

Security matters more than clever features

Turn on automatic updates, use multi-factor authentication and keep recovery information current. Never share a one-time verification code with a caller. If someone says your bank account or computer is in danger and demands immediate payment, stop and contact the company through a trusted number.

AI deserves curiosity and caution

AARP's research shows growing interest in AI among adults 50-plus, including tools for translation, home monitoring and simplifying information. At the same time, privacy and trust remain major concerns. Treat AI as a tool for assistance, not an authority that automatically knows whether a medical, financial or legal answer is correct.

Make technology work for your life

The best device is not the one with the most features. It is the one you can use confidently. Keep only the apps you need, learn the emergency features and ask for help when something feels unfamiliar.

Make the change easy to keep

The best lifestyle improvement is usually the one that fits your existing routine. Put the activity beside something you already do: a walk after breakfast, a phone call on Sunday, a security check on the first day of each month.

Avoid perfection

Healthy routines can survive an imperfect week. If you miss a walk, social event or technology task, simply restart. Consistency over months matters more than a perfect seven-day streak.

Design for real life

Lifestyle advice often fails because it assumes unlimited time, money and motivation. A useful change should work on an ordinary Tuesday, not only on an ideal day. If a new routine requires a complicated setup every morning, it is less likely to last. Make the desired behavior easy to start and easy to repeat.

Small changes can have a practical effect

Moving an item to a reachable shelf, reducing unnecessary notifications, putting a social activity on the calendar or improving lighting can remove friction from daily life. These changes may sound ordinary, but independence is often protected by a collection of ordinary decisions rather than one dramatic intervention.

Know when to ask for help

Technology, home repairs, financial security and health routines sometimes cross into areas where guessing is not sensible. Ask a qualified professional when safety, medical care, legal rights or significant money is involved. Getting help is not a sign that you cannot manage your life; it is a way of managing risk.

Bottom line

The best lifestyle advice is practical enough to use and flexible enough to survive a busy week. Choose one change, make it easy and build from there.

A practical checklist

  • Choose one change that fits your current routine.
  • Make the safer/easier option the default.
  • Review the change after two weeks.
  • Ask for help when a task involves security or safety.
  • Keep the habit simple enough to continue.
